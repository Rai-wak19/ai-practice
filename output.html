To support high-carbohydrate fueling for marathon endurance training on a strict vegan diet, this individual needs a nutrition plan focused on **easily digestible complex carbohydrates, adequate plant-based protein for muscle recovery, and sufficient calories** to match high training volumes. 

At 178 cm and 68 kg, this person has a lean frame and likely a moderate-to-high baseline caloric need depending on their weekly mileage. 

Here is a comprehensive guide on what they should eat, broken down by macronutrients, daily meal structure, and pre/during/post-run fueling.

---

### 1. Macronutrient Focus
*   **Carbohydrates (60–70% of daily calories):** Essential for maximizing glycogen stores in the liver and muscles. The focus should be on whole-grain complex carbs, with simple carbs used strategically around workouts.
*   **Protein (15–20% / approx. 1.2 to 1.4g per kg of body weight):** Around **80–95 grams per day** to repair muscle tissue from high-mileage running.
*   **Fats (15–20%):** Kept slightly lower around heavy training blocks to allow room for high carbs without causing GI distress, focusing on healthy fats like avocado, chia seeds, flax, and nuts.

---

### 2. Daily Meal Structure & Food Choices

#### **Breakfast (Pre-Morning Run or General Fueling)**
*Goal: Glycogen top-off, easy to digest.*
*   **Oatmeal** made with soy milk or water, topped with sliced banana, a tablespoon of maple syrup, and a handful of berries.
*   **Toast** with fruit preserves or almond butter and hemp seeds.
*   **Hydration:** Water with electrolytes.

#### **Lunch (Mid-Day Recovery & Sustained Energy)**
*Goal: Complex carbs, steady energy, and moderate protein.*
*   **Grain Bowl:** A large base of cooked **quinoa or brown rice**, loaded with roasted sweet potatoes (high carb/potassium), black beans or chickpeas, steamed broccoli, and a tahini-lemon dressing.
*   **Side:** A piece of fruit (like an apple or orange).

#### **Dinner (Carbohydrate Loading & Replenishment)**
*Goal: Rebuilding glycogen stores for the next day's effort.*
*   **Pasta Night:** Large portion of **durum wheat pasta** or chickpea pasta with a rich marinara sauce, lentils added to the sauce for extra protein, and nutritional yeast for B-vitamins and a cheesy flavor.
*   **Side:** Steamed asparagus or a side salad with pumpkin seeds.

#### **Snacks (Between Meals)**
*   Medjool dates stuffed with almond butter (great quick carbs).
*   Rice cakes with mashed avocado and sea salt.
*   Smoothie: Spinach, frozen mango, plant-based protein powder (pea/soy), oats, and coconut water.

---

### 3. Marathon-Specific Training Fueling

High-carb endurance training requires specific intra-workout nutrition to prevent "hitting the wall":

*   **Pre-Run (1–2 hours before long runs):** 
    *   Easily digestible simple carbs. 
    *   *Example:* White bagels with jam, a banana, or sports drinks. Avoid high fiber immediately before a hard run to prevent stomach cramps.
*   **During Runs (Runs lasting > 60–90 minutes):**
    *   Aim for 30–60+ grams of carbohydrates per hour, depending on the intensity and distance.
    *   *Vegan-friendly options:* Energy gels (check labels for vegan certification, most are), sports chews, dried fruit (dates, raisins), or homemade rice cakes with maple syrup and salt.
*   **Post-Run (Within 30–45 minutes):**
    *   A 3:1 or 4:1 ratio of carbohydrates to protein to jumpstart recovery.
    *   *Example:* A chocolate soy milk or pea-protein shake blended with a banana and berries.

---

### 4. Crucial Supplements for Vegan Runners
Because of the strict vegan restriction and high training volume, certain micronutrients need close attention:
*   **Vitamin B12:** Essential for nerve function and red blood cell production.
*   **Vitamin D3 & Calcium:** Crucial for bone density, especially important for high-impact running to prevent stress fractures.
*   **Iron:** Endurance runners are at a higher risk of iron deficiency (foot-strike hemolysis). Focus on iron-rich plant foods (spinach, lentils, pumpkin seeds) paired with Vitamin C (bell peppers, citrus) to enhance absorption. Consider regular blood panels with a doctor.
*   **Electrolytes (Sodium, Potassium, Magnesium):** Crucial for preventing cramps during long runs, especially in warmer weather.

To support heavy powerlifting training while managing a severe peanut allergy on a strict budget, your nutrition strategy should focus on **high-density, inexpensive protein sources, complex carbohydrates for lifting energy, and healthy fats that are strictly peanut-free.**

At 178 cm and 95 kg, your body requires a solid amount of fuel to recover from heavy compound lifts (squats, bench, deadlifts). Assuming a moderate activity level, a target of around **160g–190g of protein daily** is ideal. 

Here is a blueprint for your budget-friendly, peanut-free powerlifting diet:

---

### 1. The Core Budget Protein Sources (Peanut-Free)
*   **Whole Eggs:** One of the cheapest complete proteins. Great for breakfast, loaded with healthy fats and choline for recovery.
*   **Canned Tuna / Canned Salmon:** Extremely budget-friendly, high protein, and provides omega-3s to help with joint inflammation from heavy lifting.
*   **Chicken Thighs or Whole Chickens:** Chicken thighs are often cheaper than breasts, have more calories (great for lifting energy), and are very forgiving to cook. Buying a whole chicken and roasting it is usually even cheaper.
*   **Cottage Cheese or Greek Yogurt (Plain/Store Brand):** Excellent sources of slow-digesting casein protein, perfect before bed to aid muscle repair.
*   **Dried Lentils, Black Beans, and Chickpeas:** Dirt cheap. While plant protein isn't as bioavailable as meat, mixing these with rice gives you complete amino acid profiles and massive amounts of carbohydrates to fuel your workouts.
*   **Whey Protein Isolate/Concentrate (Bulk):** Look for unflavored or store-brand bulk whey. *Always check the label to ensure it is processed in a peanut-free facility.*

### 2. Safe Nut & Seed Alternatives (Replacing Peanuts)
Since peanuts are strictly off-limits (and peanut butter is the traditional budget bodybuilding staple), use these alternatives for calorie-dense fats and protein:
*   **Sunflower Seed Butter ("SunButter"):** Usually safe for peanut allergies, packed with healthy fats and calories (great if you need to gain or maintain weight).
*   **Sunflower Seeds & Pumpkin Seeds (Pepitas):** Much cheaper than almonds or cashews, great for snacking or adding to salads and rice.

### 3. Budget Carbs for Lifting Power
*   **White or Brown Rice:** Buy in 5kg or 10kg bags for the lowest cost per serving. Essential intra- and post-workout fuel.
*   **Oats (Rolled or Quick):** Inexpensive, excellent source of slow-release energy. (Check the label for cross-contamination if your allergy is airborne-severe, though most standard oats are naturally peanut-free).
*   **Bananas and Potatoes:** Cheap, potassium-rich (prevents cramping during heavy squats), and easy to digest pre-workout.

---

### Sample Daily Meal Plan (Approx. 180g Protein)

*   **Breakfast (The Lifter’s Bowl):**
    *   1 cup of oats cooked in water or milk
    *   1 scoop of peanut-free protein powder stirred in
    *   1 banana sliced on top
    *   *Cost: Very Low*

*   **Lunch (Power Rice Bowl):**
    *   200g cooked chicken thighs (or ground turkey)
    *   1.5 cups of white rice
    *   1 cup of canned black beans
    *   Salsa or hot sauce for flavor
    *   *Cost: Low*

*   **Pre-Workout Snack (1 hour before lifting):**
    *   2 slices of toast with Sunflower Seed Butter and a drizzle of honey
    *   *Cost: Low*

*   **Post-Workout Dinner:**
    *   3 whole eggs scrambled + 1 cup of egg whites (or an extra can of tuna)
    *   2 large baked potatoes with a little butter or oil
    *   Side of frozen broccoli or mixed veggies (buy frozen—they are cheaper and don't spoil)
    *   *Cost: Low*

*   **Before Bed:**
    *   1 cup of store-brand cottage cheese with a handful of sunflower seeds.
    *   *Cost: Low*

---

### Golden Rules for Shopping on a Budget with a Peanut Allergy:
1.  **Read Every Label:** Peanuts and peanut derivatives (like peanut flour or peanut oil) sneak into unexpected foods like multi-grain breads, chili sauces, and chocolate. Always read the allergen warning: *"May contain traces of peanuts"* should be avoided if your allergy is severe.
2.  **Buy Frozen and Bulk:** Buy meat on markdown and freeze it. Buy rice, oats, and beans in the largest bags available.
3.  **Hydrate:** Water is free and essential for muscle contraction and joint lubrication under heavy loads. Drink at least 3–4 liters a day.
